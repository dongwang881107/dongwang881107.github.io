This issue highlights five papers. The first two summaries are based on the titles and bibliographic records. The remaining three also draw on the abstracts. Each entry suggests questions to follow up in the paper.

## Image Reconstruction

### One-Step Rectified Flow Reconstruction with Spectral Weighting for Limited-angle Cardiac CT

*IEEE Transactions on Medical Imaging* · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/tmi.2026.3754715)

The title describes a method that combines one-step rectified flow with spectral weighting for limited-angle cardiac CT. Look for how the method uses the measured projections and whether the reported runtime covers the entire reconstruction workflow.

### A Robust Physics-Informed Implicit Neural Representation for Speed-of-Sound Image Reconstruction in USCT

*IEEE Transactions on Medical Imaging* · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/tmi.2026.3754844)

The title describes an implicit neural representation that incorporates physics to reconstruct speed-of-sound maps in ultrasound computed tomography. Check how the physical model enters the objective and which conditions vary in the robustness experiments.

## Segmentation and Registration

### Recent Advances in Medical Imaging Segmentation: Generative, Few-Shot, Foundation and Universal Models

*ACM Computing Surveys* · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3849846)

This review organizes medical image segmentation research around generative methods, few-shot learning, foundation models, and universal models. It can help readers identify where each approach is used and compare annotation needs, target modalities, and external validation.

### Bidirectional multi-scale implicit neural representation network for topology-aware lung 4D-CT registration

*Physics in Medicine & Biology* · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6560/aebd5e)

The paper uses bidirectional multiscale modeling for lung deformation over the breathing cycle. Look separately at the within-subject held-out phase evaluation, cross-subject evaluation, and external data evaluation. Check how the authors assess both landmark alignment and deformation folding.

## Quantitative Imaging

### Quantitative CT Phenotypes and Prognosis in Combined Pulmonary Fibrosis and Emphysema

*Radiology* · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1148/radiol.253493)

The study uses CT to quantify the relative extent of pulmonary fibrosis and emphysema, define two dominant phenotypes, and analyze how the phenotypes relate to prognosis. Consider the stability of segmentation and quantification and how the statistical analysis accounts for differences between participants.
