## Research problem
{: #problem }

With incomplete or noisy measurements, many images can fit nearly the same data. Grid-based Tikhonov regularization stabilizes the reconstruction by pulling it toward a reference. A coordinate network also constrains the shapes and textures the reconstruction can express. The paper asks how that function class should change as the measurement noise falls. [Authors’ open manuscript, §§2.1–2.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.19936v1#S2)

## Core contributions
{: #innovation }

1. **A second regularization parameter.** The paper treats the index of a compact class of admissible solutions as a parameter alongside the Tikhonov weight. Approximation error from that class appears explicitly in the analysis.
2. **A construction for coordinate networks.** With an architecture and bounded weights, the outputs form a compact class. A suitable family of such networks with nonnegative outputs can approximate a broader function space.
3. **A joint parameter choice.** The paper relates the penalty and representation error to noise. Under the appropriate source conditions and approximation control, the method can attain the classical order in the linear setting. [Authors’ open manuscript, Theorems 2.2, 2.5–2.6 and 3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.19936v1#S2.SS3)

## Method
{: #method }

Let x denote the image, y<sup>δ</sup> the noisy data, and F the physical operator that predicts measurements. The reconstruction minimizes the following objective over the m-th compact set Xₘ. [Authors’ open manuscript, Eq. (6)](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.19936v1#S2.SS2)

<p class="literature-equation">min<sub>x ∈ Xₘ</sub>  ‖F(x) − y<sup>δ</sup>‖² + α‖x − x*‖²</p>

- **Data fit and prior.** the first term measures the observation residual. The second penalizes distance from a reference image x*. The constraint Xₘ determines which images the solver can represent.
- **Network implementation.** spatial coordinates go in and image intensity comes out. A final ReLU enforces nonnegativity, and each observed data set drives its own parameter optimization through the forward model. The mathematical minimization and the behavior of an iterative optimizer pose separate questions.
- **Convergence trade-off.** as δ decreases, the theory reduces α while keeping δ²/α small. It also requires the squared approximation error of the compact class to vanish relative to α. A class that expands too slowly can make representation error dominate the reconstruction. [Authors’ open manuscript, §§2.2–3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.19936v1#S3)

## Experiments and results
{: #experiments }

### Edges and noise sensitivity in the open manuscript

The authors used a 128 × 128 Shepp–Logan phantom, simulated 50 projection angles under the standard Radon transform, and added white Gaussian noise. Figure 2 compares reconstructions at input SNRs of about 42.60, 23.10, and 16.58 dB. The network has four hidden layers of 100 units each, with Adam fitting each observation. The closed-form Tikhonov baseline searches the unconstrained grid space, while the network enforces nonnegativity. The comparison reflects both the representation and the constraints. The network preserves some edges sharply and smooths away some small structures near the bottom of the phantom. [Authors’ open manuscript, §§4.1–4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.19936v1#S4)

### Attenuation and additional baselines in the journal supplement

The supplement extends the comparisons to Shepp–Logan and walnut images, standard Radon and the authors’ attenuated model, and three methods. The baselines are nonnegative Tikhonov and a Plug-and-Play (PnP) method with a DnCNN denoiser. The full settings use 25 noise realizations per condition at 20, 25 and 30 dB. The authors report ℓ₂ error, PSNR and SSIM. [Journal supplement, §§SM10–SM12](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/suppl/10.1137/25M1762774/suppl_file/supp_mat.pdf)

The redraw below uses the **five displayed, paired noise realizations** for attenuated Radon and a walnut image at **20 dB** from Fig. SM68. In those five cases, mean ℓ₂ errors are 14.62 for Tikhonov, 11.91 for PnP and 10.80 for the coordinate MLP. The MLP has lower ℓ₂ error and higher SSIM in all five displayed examples. The complete supplement shows the results across other conditions. [Journal supplement, Fig. SM68, printed p. ## Conclusions and limitations
{: #conclusions }

The contribution is a framework for choosing the network’s solution class together with the penalty. The simulations show where that combination helps reconstruction and where fine detail or noise stability can still be lost. The supplementary experiments use known ground truth to select parameters. Applying the method to real measurements calls for a selection rule that can work without ground truth and a check for sensitivity to forward-model errors. [Authors’ open manuscript, §§4.2–5](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.19936v1#S5) · [Journal supplement, §SM10](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/suppl/10.1137/25M1762774/suppl_file/supp_mat.pdf)

## Future directions
{: #future }

The authors suggest evaluating more object morphologies. For electrical impedance tomography, three checks would make that question concrete. [Authors’ open manuscript, §5](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.19936v1#S5)

1. **Separate noise and model mismatch.** Compare grid-based Tikhonov and a constrained coordinate network on the same simulated conductivity phantoms. Vary voltage noise separately from electrode-position and contact-impedance errors.
2. **Select both parameters on equal measurements.** Estimate noise from repeated measurements. Use held-out excitation patterns to select the network constraint and penalty weight, and reserve a separate set of excitation patterns to report test voltage error.
3. **Evaluate conductivity and small targets.** Record held-out voltage error, conductivity bias in the target region, and boundary recovery. If voltage prediction improves while conductivity bias persists, investigate identifiability and forward-model error.
