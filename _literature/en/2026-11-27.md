On a handheld color photograph of an infant’s retina, the major vessels may be visible while a peripheral area of poor perfusion remains hard to judge. An angiogram follows injected fluorescein through the vessels and can reveal abnormalities that the photograph only hints at. Yingpeng Xie and colleagues asked whether a model could generate an angiography-like view from the photograph alone and whether that view would help readers classify disease. One example in the supplement sets the boundary of the question. Given the same color photograph, the recorded angiogram looks different in the early and late phases, while the model generates a single image. [Article in Press, pp. 1–2](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-026-03462-z_reference.pdf#page=1) · [Supplementary Fig. S4](https://static-content.springer.com/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41746-026-03462-z/MediaObjects/41746_2026_3462_MOESM1_ESM.pdf#page=4)

## Research problem
{: #problem }

Color fundus photography (CFP) shows retinal morphology. Fundus fluorescein angiography (FFA) records how dye moves, where it fails to perfuse and where it leaks. Learning a mapping between them poses two problems. First, changes in camera angle, field of view and contact pressure during separate handheld acquisitions create local deformations even after aligning the frames. Pixel-by-pixel supervision can therefore penalize a vessel that appears in approximately the correct place. Second, a static photograph does not contain every functional feature that determines fluorescence at a particular time. That uncertainty remains even with exact registration. [Article in Press, Introduction and Discussion, pp. 1–2 and 7–9](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-026-03462-z_reference.pdf#page=7)

## Core contributions
{: #innovation }

1. **Build weakly paired supervision from handheld acquisitions.** The authors align views from the same clinical visit, filter pairs with insufficient geometric support and keep the shared retinal field. [Fig. 1 and registration Methods, pp. 3 and 10–11](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-026-03462-z_reference.pdf#page=10)
2. **Start generation from the source photograph’s latent representation.** InfantAngio adapts a pretrained diffusion model to follow a deterministic trajectory from encoded CFP to an FFA-like representation. A retinal foundation model supplies visual features that condition the trajectory at each step. [Methods, Eqs. (1)–(7), pp. 11–13](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-026-03462-z_reference.pdf#page=11)
3. **Test image appearance and downstream decisions separately.** The study combines image similarity metrics, blinded assessment of image origin and quality, a classifier benchmark, and a comparison of clinicians’ diagnostic decisions with and without the generated view. [Results, pp. 3–8](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-026-03462-z_reference.pdf#page=6)

## Method
{: #method }

### What does a registered pair represent?

MINIMA combines SuperPoint keypoints with LightGlue matching. RANSAC fits a homography, after which the authors retain only the overlap between the retinal masks. Pairs with fewer than 15 inlier matches or less than 50% overlap relative to the source image area are excluded. The retained pairs have a median 38 inliers and a median overlap ratio of 0.664. A homography handles global perspective changes, while pressure-induced deformation and other local differences remain. The comparison methods use the same registered data. [Registration Methods, pp. 10–11](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-026-03462-z_reference.pdf#page=11) · [Supplementary Fig. S3 and Table S1](https://static-content.springer.com/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41746-026-03462-z/MediaObjects/41746_2026_3462_MOESM1_ESM.pdf#page=3)

The 83,950 registered pair records came from 259 infants at one hospital. Each contributing color image matched a median of seven FFA frames, and each contributing FFA frame matched a median of five color images. Records can share the same visit, eye or image. The authors kept infants together when dividing the data. The generator’s development set contained 209 infants and 73,691 pairs, and the evaluation set contained 50 different infants and 10,259 pairs. The latter was subsequently split again by infant, 70% for classifier development and 30% for classifier testing. These partitions prevent the illustrated many-to-many matching from placing records from the same child on both sides of either split. [Data acquisition and implementation, pp. 10 and 14](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-026-03462-z_reference.pdf#page=10) · [Classifier setup](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-026-03462-z_reference.pdf#page=14)

At inference, the denoising diffusion implicit model (DDIM) scheduler uses 50 deterministic updates to estimate an FFA-like latent and the decoder converts it into an image. In parallel, a RETFound encoder extracts retinal features from the CFP, and an attention-based resampler condenses them into 16 prompts for the U-Net. ## Experiments and results
{: #experiments }

### Are the images more similar to the reference?

Across five infantile diagnostic categories, mean Fréchet inception distance (FID) was 62.20 for InfantAngio and 72.76 for InstructPix2pix, the best comparison by mean FID in Table 2. That is a 14.5% relative decrease. The paired perceptual similarity metric LPIPS was 0.371 for InfantAngio and 0.372 for UWAT-GAN. The ablation in Table 3 makes the trade-off clearer. Starting from the stochastic diffusion control, deterministic transport improved FID from 72.76 to 64.26. Adding the retinal encoder improved it again to 62.20 while feature-space recall fell from 0.980 to 0.929. The generated images became closer to the reference distribution on one metric while covering less of its range on another. This recall measures feature-distribution coverage. Clinical sensitivity for a lesion or a screening decision is evaluated separately. [Results, Table 2 and Table 3, pp. 4 and 6](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-026-03462-z_reference.pdf#page=4) · [Metric definitions, p. 14](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-026-03462-z_reference.pdf#page=14)

Four ophthalmologists judged image origin in a blinded pool of 100 FFA images, evenly divided between real and generated images and balanced by category at the image level. They identified origin with 49.8% overall accuracy. The mean quality ratings were 3.79 for real and 3.71 for generated images on a five-point scale (p = 0.347). Some images could come from the same child or the same case. The results describe perceived realism and quality in this pool. They leave open how often an individual generated lesion agrees with the corresponding angiogram. [Figs. 7–8 and Methods, pp. 6, 9–10 and 14](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-026-03462-z_reference.pdf#page=6)

### Does a second view improve classification?

The downstream classifier’s accuracy was 89.5% (95% CI 88.1–90.9%) with CFP alone, 92.8% (91.5–93.9%) with CFP plus generated FFA, and 93.2% (92.0–94.3%) with CFP plus recorded FFA. Macro-AUC was 0.986, 0.989 and 0.990, respectively. Table 4 reports p &lt; 0.01 for each dual-input AUC comparison with CFP alone, using paired patient-cluster bootstrap. The recorded-FFA comparison is a reference for this benchmark. Direct equivalence between the two FFA inputs was outside the reported tests. The dual-input classifiers also used two encoders. A capacity-matched control with a second representation of the same CFP would help establish how much of the gain is specific to the synthesized angiography-like representation. [Table 4, p. 11](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-026-03462-z_reference.pdf#page=11) · [Implementation and statistical analysis, pp. 14–15](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-026-03462-z_reference.pdf#page=14)

### Did ophthalmologists make different decisions?

In a separate retrospective task, the same four ophthalmologists categorized the same 50 color images with CFP alone and with the generated view alongside it. Each of the five diagnostic categories contributed ten images. Pooled accuracy rose from 79.0% to 88.0% (p &lt; 0.01 by patient-cluster permutation), and Fleiss’ κ rose from 0.668 to 0.800. Within the ROP subset, sensitivity for referable disease rose from 62.5% to 77.5%, while specificity moved from 87.5% to 90.0%. The resident gained 18 percentage points in overall accuracy and one attending’s accuracy was unchanged. The two conditions yielded repeated judgments on a balanced set of 50 images. Condition order and washout are not specified in the accepted Methods, so the extent to which readers recognized items across conditions is unclear. These figures describe the task’s image set and reader panel. [Fig. 10, p. 13](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-026-03462-z_reference.pdf#page=13) · [Reader Methods and patient-cluster test, pp. 14–15](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-026-03462-z_reference.pdf#page=14)

## Conclusions and limitations
{: #conclusions }

In this single-center retrospective study, an angiography-like representation made some diagnostic distinctions easier for a classifier and a small panel of ophthalmologists. The supplement also shows two concrete failure modes. One color photograph corresponds to different early and late angiographic appearances. In another case, the generated image adds punctate hyperfluorescence at dense hard exudates where the reference image shows no corresponding signal. These examples concern the evidence behind individual findings. A plausible image and a correct category can coexist with an inaccurate local representation. The adult MPOS experiment trained the method on a separate adult training set. It shows that the architecture can be retrained in a different acquisition setting, while transfer of the infant-trained model to a different hospital or camera remains untested in the study. [Discussion, pp. 7–9](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-026-03462-z_reference.pdf#page=7) · [Supplementary Fig. S4](https://static-content.springer.com/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41746-026-03462-z/MediaObjects/41746_2026_3462_MOESM1_ESM.pdf#page=4)

## Future directions
{: #future }

The authors propose phase-conditioned synthesis and a lesion-level audit with experts, alongside prospective validation. A focused study could use time-labeled angiograms when they are already collected, ask masked graders to mark leakage, nonperfusion and new vessels, then count missed and unsupported findings by lesion and phase. A separate prospective reader study could randomize condition order, separate repeat readings and include external centers and cameras. Its primary endpoint could be referable ROP sensitivity at a prespecified specificity, with uncertainty reported by infant and reader. The study could then examine where the generated view changes a screening decision and where the photographs leave too little information for a reliable angiographic interpretation. [Discussion, pp. 7–9](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-026-03462-z_reference.pdf#page=7)
