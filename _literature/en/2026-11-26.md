Imagine inspecting a knee section and seeing a meniscus that still covers much of the tibial surface. Move the imaging plane a few hundred micrometers and the same meniscus may have shifted away from a focal cartilage lesion. Which section should represent the joint? Mingyu Tian, Haoyu Gong and colleagues developed a way to photograph the exposed face of a frozen mouse knee, mill away a thin layer and repeat. The resulting volume retains the positions of cartilage, meniscus and bone in the same coordinate system, so readers can follow a lesion across planes and measure each structure in relation to its neighbors. [Published paper, Figs. 1 and 3](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s43074-026-00297-5#Fig1)

This *PhotoniX* study was published online on November 24, 2026, and selected from the November 24 curated literature digest. The analysis draws on the publisher's full text, supplementary figures and Methods. ## Research problem
{: #problem }

Osteoarthritis changes the osteochondral unit, which spans non-calcified cartilage, calcified cartilage and subchondral bone. It can also displace the meniscus and produce new growths at the joint margin, called abaxial osteophytes. Those changes need to be located together. Routine stained histology provides detailed tissue contrast, but the choice of section affects which lesions are visible, and decalcification removes mineral information. Standard micro-CT maps mineralized structures well, while soft-tissue delineation depends on the acquisition and contrast protocol. The authors set out to reconstruct the whole joint with enough contrast to see the boundary between adjacent tissues while preserving their original spatial relationships. [Published paper, Introduction and Figs. 1–3](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s43074-026-00297-5#Sec1)

## Core contributions
{: #innovation }

1. **Make cryogenic block-face imaging work across tissue interfaces.** The team paired milling with native green autofluorescence and a line-confocal detector. This separates structures such as non-calcified cartilage, calcified cartilage and subchondral bone on one image, including the tidemark and cement line between those layers. [Published paper, Fig. 1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s43074-026-00297-5#Fig1)
2. **Automate the repeated scan–mill cycle.** The instrument moves the specimen between imaging and cutting stations, maintains cooling and clears frost or debris before the next acquisition. This makes continuous whole-joint acquisition practical at approximately 200 coronal levels per joint. [Published paper, Methods](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s43074-026-00297-5#Sec20)
3. **Measure the geometry between tissues.** The authors combined volume, lesion grade and the fraction of a tibial reference area overlapped by the projected meniscus. Meniscal enlargement and reduced coverage can coexist. This distinction would be lost if the analysis used volume alone. [Published paper, Figs. 6–7](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s43074-026-00297-5#Fig7)

## Method
{: #method }

### From a frozen block face to a registered volume

In an initial wide-field comparison, the green emission band centered at 536 nm with a 40 nm bandwidth gave the clearest separation among the bands tested. Excitation at 405, 434 and 445 nm produced similar boundary profiles within that green band in the tested knee, so the line-confocal system used an available 405 nm laser. A cylindrical lens forms a line of illumination. The camera retains its central pixel row as a 1.625 μm effective virtual slit at the specimen plane. Signals from above or below the focal plane spread more across camera rows and contribute less to the retained row. Strip images are corrected, stitched and registered as the instrument scans the exposed surface. After imaging, the cutter removes 20 μm of tissue to expose the next face. Automated liquid-nitrogen replenishment and spraying keep the exposed surface cooled and clear of residue. [Published paper, Methods](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s43074-026-00297-5#Sec20) · [Supplementary Figs. 1, 3–4 and 8](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s43074-026-00297-5#Sec28)

The measured full widths at half maximum from five fluorescent beads were 2.44 ± 0.09 μm and 1.98 ± 0.02 μm across the two lateral axes and 15.95 ± 0.57 μm axially (means ± SEM). The axial optical response and the 20 μm spacing at which the actual joint is sampled describe different parts of the acquisition. Together they make this a strongly anisotropic volume. The authors report less than 30 minutes of preparation before imaging, about 10 hours to image two staggered joints, and about six hours per specimen for subsequent reconstruction, manual soft-tissue segmentation and volumetric analysis. [Published paper, Methods](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s43074-026-00297-5#Sec20) · [Resolution measurement](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s43074-026-00297-5#Sec21)

Including osteophytes can increase coverage when the newly added region has a larger overlap fraction than the original calcified-cartilage region. It enlarges both the potential overlap and the reference area. Both measures quantify two-dimensional geometric overlap. Estimating actual contact under load would require additional measurements. ## Experiments and results
{: #experiments }

### Can native fluorescence delineate boundaries between tissues?

Figure 1F shows whether the tidemark and cement line can be traced across the osteochondral unit. In the representative comparison, the signal-to-background ratio increased from 3.08 for the wide-field system to 31.79 for the line-confocal system. Both systems used the same emission filter, but their excitation sources differed. The virtual-slit test in Supplementary Fig. 8 compared two reconstructions from the same acquisition. In that knee image, retaining one row gave steeper transitions and increased the signal-to-background ratio from 25.78 to 32.69 compared with the simulated wide field. Its contrast-to-noise ratio decreased slightly, from 62.12 to 59.56. This control supports better boundary delineation in that example and clarifies why image quality needs more than one metric. [Published paper, Fig. 1 and Methods](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s43074-026-00297-5#Fig1) · [Same-acquisition comparison](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s43074-026-00297-5#Sec20)

Figure 2 compares the right knee of one mouse in vivo and ex vivo with micro-CT, and after freezing with LC cryo-MOST. Fine trabeculae appeared in ex vivo micro-CT and LC cryo-MOST at comparable locations; the latter also delineated adjacent cartilage and ligament under the conditions tested. ### What does a whole joint reveal that a sampled section can miss?

The osteoarthritis model destabilized the medial meniscus (DMM). The researchers compared DMM and sham-operated groups at 2, 5 and 10 weeks, using separate animals at each endpoint. In representative sections, an early anterior meniscal displacement was spatially distinct from later hypertrophy in central regions and from focal cartilage damage. In one representative 5-week DMM joint, serial sections showed that an apparently localized cartilage lesion reached the calcified-cartilage interface at deeper positions. The score changed from grade 2 to grade 3. Three blinded scorers reviewed the complete volumes and selected the most affected plane for each lesion. Their individual-scoring ICCs ranged from 0.846 to 0.973 across the seven scoring categories. In the DMM groups, the medial tibial cartilage score rose from 2.00 ± 0.24 at week 2 to 3.40 ± 0.37 at week 5 and 4.93 ± 0.46 at week 10. This scale locates and grades peak damage, while the volumetric analysis measures how much tissue remains in a prescribed region. [Published paper, Fig. 4 and Table 2](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s43074-026-00297-5#Fig4) · [Scoring and Supplementary Fig. 5 example](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s43074-026-00297-5#Sec26)

In the medial tibial plateau of the DMM groups, non-calcified cartilage in the central weight-bearing region fell from 0.0216 ± 0.0006 mm³ at week 2 to 0.0140 ± 0.0031 mm³ at week 10. This is a 35.19% decrease (p = 0.043). Over those endpoints, trabecular bone volume fraction increased from 48.35% ± 2.33% to 58.59% ± 2.54% (p = 0.018), and abaxial osteophyte volume increased from 0.0781 ± 0.0233 to 0.1675 ± 0.0286 mm³ (p = 0.042). The study reports means ± SEM and five imaged mice per arm and time point for these outcomes. The three measurements show cartilage loss, trabecular sclerosis and growth at the joint margin in different parts of the volume. [Published paper, Figs. 5–6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s43074-026-00297-5#Fig5) · [Soft-tissue results](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s43074-026-00297-5#Sec8)

### Does a larger meniscus cover more of the tibial plateau?

In the DMM medial compartment, projected coverage relative to calcified cartilage decreased from 49.78% ± 4.25% at week 2 to 36.56% ± 2.93% at week 5 (p = 0.034). At week 5 it was below the sham group’s 51.75% ± 1.89% (p = 0.002). By week 10 the DMM group’s coverage was 39.33% ± 2.84%, with no significant difference from week 5. Meanwhile, medial meniscal volume increased from 0.252 ± 0.027 mm³ at week 5 to 0.645 ± 0.111 mm³ at week 10 (p = 0.009). In the paper’s medial correlation analysis, the association of the cartilage-reference coverage with meniscal volume was negative (Spearman <span class="literature-math">r<sub>s</sub></span> = −0.430, p = 0.018). The lateral association was positive (<span class="literature-math">r<sub>s</sub></span> = 0.689, p &lt; 0.001). The corresponding medial associations no longer reached statistical significance when osteophytes were included in the reference area. This dependence on the compartment and measurement definition is important when interpreting the correlations. [Published paper, Figs. 6D and 7](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s43074-026-00297-5#Fig7)

## Conclusions and limitations
{: #conclusions }

LC cryo-MOST demonstrates that cryogenically preserved whole mouse knees can yield registered three-dimensional measurements of soft and hard tissues without chemical fixation, decalcification or added fluorescent labels in the main imaging workflow. The DMM experiment shows what this combined view adds to a single section or a volume measurement alone. The single fluorescence channel combines signals from several possible endogenous sources, so the investigators interpreted intensity together with morphology and anatomical location. The technique uses terminal milling, and postoperative changes in this study were compared across different animals at each time point. Sample sizes were small and based on field practice without a formal power calculation. The reported t-tests and correlations are best interpreted as exploratory evidence for this mouse model. [Published paper, Discussion and statistical analysis](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s43074-026-00297-5#Sec9) · [Statistical analysis](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s43074-026-00297-5#Sec27)

Automated bone segmentation agreed closely with manual labels on tested images. For the independent healthy knee, the reported Dice coefficient was 97.04% ± 0.44% (mean ± SD). For the held-out 10-week DMM knee, the fine-tuned network yielded 94.30% ± 3.98%. These figures capture agreement for images from the two individual test specimens. The DMM evaluation used five image planes spaced 600 μm apart in that one knee. An external cohort and observer variability tests would be needed for broader accuracy estimates, especially for manually outlined soft tissues and interfaces with weak contrast. [Published paper, Fig. 5 and segmentation Methods](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s43074-026-00297-5#Fig5) · [Segmentation Methods](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s43074-026-00297-5#Sec24)

## Future directions
{: #future }

The authors propose combining serial in vivo micro-CT bone measurements with terminal LC cryo-MOST through spatial registration. They also suggest further spectral channels, independent matched histological or chemical references, and automated soft-tissue segmentation. These developments would address different uncertainties. Registration would align time points, spectral validation would clarify the origin of contrast, and segmentation validation would test reproducibility of the geometric measurements. [Published paper, Discussion](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s43074-026-00297-5#Sec9)

A follow-up study could prespecify whether meniscal coverage is referenced to calcified cartilage alone or includes osteophytes, then vary the projection plane and segmentation contours to quantify sensitivity to those choices. An independent mechanical measurement in loaded joints could then test whether a change in projected coverage predicts a change in contact area or load sharing. An analogous design in quantitative electrical impedance tomography would register a reconstruction to a known phantom geometry and ask how a derived overlap or volume endpoint changes under plausible perturbations of the segmentation or measurement setup. [Published paper, geometric definitions and discussion](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s43074-026-00297-5#Sec23)
